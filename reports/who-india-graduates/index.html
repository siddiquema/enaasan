Who India Graduates

How many students leave Indian colleges and universities each year with a degree in Arts, Science, Commerce or Engineering? This report answers that using only the Government of India's own published records.

Headline finding
5.5×

For every engineering graduate, India's colleges produce about five and a half graduates in Arts, Science or Commerce.

Arts, Science and Commerce together account for roughly 59% of India's undergraduate graduates. Engineering accounts for about 11%. Between the two latest surveys, graduate numbers in all three general streams fell while Engineering and Medical Science grew. Enrolment data suggests that shift will continue: Engineering has now overtaken Commerce in its share of undergraduate enrolment.

All figures come from the All India Survey on Higher Education (AISHE). The 2023-24 survey counts students who graduated in academic year 2022-23. Out-turn figures are based on the institutions that responded (about 92%), so true national totals are somewhat higher. Methodology and every source are at the end of this page.

Findings

Finding 1 Data

Arts alone produces nearly three times as many graduates as Engineering

At undergraduate level, Arts produced 24.4 lakh graduates in AISHE 2023-24. Science followed with 11.4 lakh, Commerce with 10.1 lakh and Engineering & Technology with 8.3 lakh. Education and Medical Science were the next largest.

undergraduates graduated in Arts, about 2.9 times Engineering's 8.3 lakh
Source [1]

By degree programme rather than discipline, the B.A. (including B.A. Hons) was awarded to 28.6 lakh students, 36.8% of all undergraduate out-turn. The B.Sc. went to 13.1 lakh and the B.Com. to 10.1 lakh. B.E. and B.Tech. together went to 8.2 lakh.

Finding 2 Data

General-stream graduate numbers fell in one year while Engineering grew

Comparing the two latest surveys, released together on 8 July 2026, graduate numbers fell in Arts, Science and Commerce. Engineering, Education and Medical Science rose. Total out-turn across all levels still edged up, from 1.09 crore to 1.10 crore.

Read these one-year changes with care. The set of institutions that respond differs slightly between surveys, so part of any change can come from who reported rather than from real shifts. Two data points show a direction, not yet a trend.

Finding 3 Data

The pipeline is tilting: Engineering has overtaken Commerce in enrolment

Out-turn shows who graduated. Enrolment shows who is still in the system. In AISHE 2022-23, Commerce held a larger share of undergraduate enrolment than Engineering. In AISHE 2023-24, the order reversed. Arts still enrols nearly a third of all undergraduates, but its share fell by about two percentage points in a year.

share of UG enrolment, Engineering vs Commerce, AISHE 2023-24

Within Engineering, computing dominates. Computer Engineering accounts for 18.4 lakh of the 46.3 lakh students enrolled in Engineering & Technology across all levels, about two in five (AISHE 2023-24, section 2.2.7.1 [1]).

Finding 4 Data

Women are the majority of graduates in every general stream

Women made up 52.1% of all pass-outs in AISHE 2023-24. They were 55.2% of Arts graduates, 53.9% of Science graduates and 51.5% of Commerce graduates. Engineering is the exception: women are 31.1% of undergraduate Engineering enrolment. AISHE does not publish the female share of Engineering out-turn in its report text.

Where students and parents can start

These numbers describe supply: how many people hold each kind of degree. They do not say which degree leads to a job. Still, supply matters when choosing a stream.

  • A popular degree means more competition. A B.A. or B.Sc. graduate enters a pool of lakhs of people with the same qualification. Plan early for what will set you apart: a specialisation, a skill, internships or a second language. Communication skills guide
  • Look at the subject, not only the stream. "Arts" covers economics, psychology, languages and history. "Engineering" is now mostly computing. Ask what is studied inside the label. Stream Explorer
  • Check the trend before you commit. A stream that is growing today will produce more graduates four years from now, when you finish. Career Compass
  • Use official data. AISHE tables are free on aishe.gov.in, including state-wise figures for your own state. Parents: Guidance Hub

Methodology & sources

Method

This report uses only figures published by the Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education, Government of India, in the AISHE Final Reports for 2022-23 and 2023-24, released together on 8 July 2026. No figures were estimated or modelled. Where this report shows a derived number, it is simple arithmetic on published figures:

  • Undergraduate out-turn (≈78 lakh): 71.0% × 1.10 crore. The report states the share, not the exact count.
  • Ratio of 5.5: (24.4 + 11.4 + 10.1) ÷ 8.3 lakh, UG discipline-wise out-turn, 2023-24.
  • "Nearly three times": 24.4 ÷ 8.3 = 2.9.
  • Shares of 59% and 11%: discipline out-turn divided by the derived UG total.
  • Changes between surveys: differences between the two reports' discipline-wise figures.

Limitations

  • AISHE is voluntary and self-reported by institutions. In 2023-24, 59,553 of 64,756 registered institutions took part (about 92%).
  • Out-turn figures are "based on actual response" and are not adjusted for non-responding institutions. Enrolment totals are estimated by AISHE; discipline shares are not.
  • Each survey's out-turn refers to the previous academic year. AISHE 2023-24 counts students who graduated in AY 2022-23.
  • Discipline and programme differ. "Arts" (24.4 lakh) is a discipline group. The B.A. degree (28.6 lakh) is a programme and includes B.A. students counted under other disciplines. We use discipline figures for comparisons and programme figures only where labelled. The 2023-24 Key Results page gives 24.4 lakh for the B.A.; we follow the detailed section 2.7.3, which gives 28.6 lakh.
  • AISHE measures education, not employment. This report makes no claim about jobs or employability.
